UK public estate decarbonisation screening and evaluation

Draft v0.2, 20 November 2026. This revision adds nation-relative deciles 1 to 5 and bands D to G as the first screening filters. The method supports screening for opportunities with no active delivery investment, tracking funded work, evaluating completed work and prioritising evidence checks. The only worked site is Mile End Hospital within the Tower Hamlets pilot. The Tower Hamlets screening method governs local validation. The earlier hypothesis about missing energy evidence still awaits verbatim confirmation.

1. Define the population and evidence boundaries

Include current public-facing non-domestic premises delivering services such as healthcare, culture, community services or justice within the approved study boundary. Include a mixed-use building only for its identifiable in-scope part. Record public function, operator, tenure, reporting date and scope. Retain excluded or undecided records with reasons such as dwelling, closed service, back office, out of area, aggregate only or unresolved premises.

Use a verified Unique Property Reference Number (UPRN), nation, estate owner or operator identifier, local site reference and dated building or demise scope for linkage. A UPRN identifies an addressable object. It can be shared by site records, represent a campus or parent building, or differ from the metering and certificate boundaries. Keep an unresolved match in the evidence queue. Use an authorised address source and one-to-many review before accepting any join. Record source identifier, date, confidence, competing matches and reviewer. Tower Hamlets identity and join rules

Deprivation and energy rating filters

  • Deprivation. Select deciles 1 to 5 inclusive, where 1 is the most deprived. This is the more deprived half of small areas within each nation by rank, subject to the publisher’s grouping convention. It is a share of small areas, with no fixed share of people or assets. Use the nation’s overall index as the provisional primary screen. Retain separate England IMD, IDACI and IDAOPI threshold flags and counts. For the other nations, add supplementary indicators only where their published scope, population and method suit the same question. Keep those results separate and document comparability before making any cross-nation comparison. A supplementary flag can prompt investigation of service-user needs. For the initial screen it does not change the overall-index result. Record the release, area code and vintage, match status, scale direction and denominator. If a release gives ranks without deciles, use an official published mapping where available. Otherwise document and agree the conversion, handling of ties and boundary rounding before applying it. Keep unmatched or ambiguous locations in the evidence queue.
  • Energy rating. D or worse means letter bands D, E, F or G. Run separate screens for a current non-domestic asset EPC and a current operational DEC where applicable and comparable. Confirm the current register status, reference, issue and expiry dates, scope match, and the DEC measurement period where available. Record EPC and DEC eligibility, search coverage, distinct premises and D–G results by type and nation, alongside A–C and separate states for expired, conflicting, unmatched, absent, unavailable, out-of-scope and unverified evidence. Record the rating scale and method used. Treat a D–G band as a screening lead. Physical energy use, cost, carbon and feasible savings require their own evidence.
  • Combine only at the assessed scope. A positive rating screen for a certificate type requires both the primary geography flag and a verified comparable D–G rating for the relevant premises. Keep supplementary results and separate EPC and DEC outcomes visible. Apply investment and evidence queues at site-and-measure scope independently of the rating. A screen can flag a funded project, an opportunity with confirmed no active investment, or a need to verify evidence. Backlog and spend remain separate decision inputs.

3. Record investment by site and measure

For every proposed, active and historic project record the matched site or part, system and measure scope, actor, provenance and discovery route. Check relevant government awards, local funds, procurement notices and the owner’s capital, maintenance and programme records. Include self-funded and co-funded projects. Public lists provide leads. Request or reuse owner confirmation only under an authorised route. Record each source checked, scope, search date, lookback, identifiers and held or unavailable records. A search with no public award found gives only the result of that search.

Review changes against the evidence effective at the as-of date. An announced or conditional award without approved delivery belongs in a development watchlist. Feasibility funding remains separately visible. A grant can support delivery without proving work started or establishing completion. Use verified actual commissioning or operation for entry to evaluation. Expired checks, absent coverage and unresolved conflicts return to unknown, with the original observations retained. Propose a lookback and recertification interval by source and programme for reviewer approval. Ordinary evidence checks can proceed while the intervals are agreed.

Avoid adding programme-wide awards to site awards or counting a co-funded project twice. Match recipient, scope, award IDs and dates before rolling up. Allocate only evidenced shares. Keep unidentified overlaps unresolved. Flag a building with any known current work. A separately defined measure outside that work can be reviewed for the opportunity shortlist with affirmative confirmation for that measure and its scope.

4. Assign a queue deterministically

The selection unit is one assessed premises, one defined candidate measure and one as-of date. Evaluate the rules in order and use the first applicable row. Maintain site-wide investment flags and a history of other projects separately. Classification thresholds, data sufficiency and screening windows are draft policy choices to approve before ranking.

Condition in order Queue and treatment
Evidence shows out of scope Retain the exclusion, reason and date.
Identity, measure scope or a conflicting stage claim is unresolved Needs verification. Retain known building work flags and conflicting or expired evidence while resolving the case.
Relevant approved or committed delivery, work underway, disruption or commissioning Investment pipeline. Keep funding evidence, baseline readiness and milestones. Hold that measure out of the no-active-investment shortlist.
Relevant work is confirmed operational or completed Later outcome evaluation. Record historic or recent completion and data readiness. Quarantine non-comparable periods and wait for stable follow-up where needed. A different unaddressed measure gets its own assessment.
Application, feasibility or announced potential award, with delivery unconfirmed Needs verification, with a development flag. Record the grant phase. If the records independently confirm no active delivery for a separately defined measure, apply the next rule to that measure.
Affirmative, in-date confirmation of no active relevant delivery, and the assessed measure meets the deprivation and rating screen and comparable energy evidence criteria No-active-investment shortlist for the assessed measure and scope. Any unrelated current work stays visible in the building flag.
Affirmative no active relevant delivery, with energy screening evidence insufficient Needs verification. Keep the investment confirmation and specify the missing energy evidence.
Remaining cases including a public search with no award, or a cancelled project with current evidence gaps Needs verification. A cancelled project can be recorded as inactive. Apply the shortlist rule only after checking for replacement work and confirming current no-active status.

Keep annual hard FM or routine repairs, soft FM, utility bills and metered consumption, decarbonisation capital, and estimated backlog and risk as separate measures. Backlog estimates describe deferred work. Safety or service-critical escalation remains visible in its own queue, without altering an energy or carbon rank. Where maintenance explicitly includes decarbonisation, tag the evidenced measure and cost portion and avoid double counting. Annual bills and realised carbon are based on observed energy and prices. Do not offset them with backlog, maintenance, capital awards or estimated savings.

Treat major repair, planned shutdown, commissioning, PV, changed service activity and new or changed meter coverage as possible changes to comparability. Declare affected periods ineligible for comparison or use a justified non-routine adjustment and report its effect and uncertainty.

5. Freeze the baseline and evaluate delivered work

  1. Before works. Record the measure theory, system and meter boundary, surveyed floor-area basis and comparator or controls. Freeze the baseline period, input hashes, quality checks, adjustment model and reporting design. Use matched 12-month baseline and follow-up windows as a proposed default where stable data are available. Approve another window only with a clear seasonal and scope rationale.
  2. During works. Record actual installation, commissioning and service changes. Track concurrent capital, maintenance, building extension, clinical or community service changes and disruptions. Record actual outturn separately from project value and funding approvals.
  3. After stable operation. Obtain complete monthly data for gas, electricity, heat and any other material fuel, with actual or estimated reads, floor area and meter coverage. Record heating degree days from a documented weather series, service hours, occupancy or activity and changes in relevant loads. Capture actual invoices, tariffs, standing and other charges. Match scope and periods across fuels and bills.
  4. Calculate separate outcomes. Keep fuel-specific kWh, measured-area intensity and raw utility spend. Estimate a comparable baseline under follow-up weather and usage using a documented model where supported. Compare adjusted baseline with follow-up and show raw results, uncertainty and boundaries. For PV, retain grid imports, generation, exports and self-use. In a simple site without material storage flows, electricity demand equals imports plus on-site generation used on site. Account for storage and other flows where present. A reduction in grid imports alone does not establish lower site demand. For heat pumps, assess gas reduction and electricity increases together. Report actual bill movement and cost change at a common tariff separately, with the treatment of fixed charges documented.
  5. Calculate carbon consistently. Calculate operational tonnes CO₂e by multiplying each fuel and electricity activity by its applicable factor and converting units. Document the boundary, geographic applicability, factor series, year and reporting basis. Use a frozen common factor set when isolating the effect of changes in activity. Use year-specific factors for realised emissions in each reporting year. Show these results separately and record the treatment of exports, electricity accounting basis and any out-of-scope emissions.
  6. State strength of evidence. Use distinct labels for anticipated or modelled savings, observed change, adjusted change, and causally attributable impact. For causal attribution use a credible comparator or pre-specified evaluation design with checks on baseline trends, selection, concurrent work and service changes. If overlapping works cannot be disentangled, evaluate their package with a qualification. If a robust counterfactual is unavailable, describe the adjusted change and plausible competing explanations. Leave savings unclaimed when monitoring data or essential scope checks are missing.

7. Test the rules before applying them elsewhere

Use the following hypothetical cases to test queue outcomes. They are expected outcomes for review and do not represent additional sites.

Test case Expected outcome
Certified premises, verified comparable baseline and current owner confirmation of no relevant delivery after checks Shortlist the eligible measure if it meets the approved criteria.
No award found and owner’s records unavailable Needs verification. The search does not establish absence of active investment.
Feasibility-only award Development flag and needs verification for delivery. Keep the funding type distinct.

Generate a versioned coverage table by nation, source, facility type and investment stage. Report counts and denominators for discovered, eligible, scope verified, search complete, no active investment confirmed, in pipeline, evaluation ready, out of scope and unknown. Include a site view and a measure view to avoid implying that measure totals count sites. Keep the evidence check queue separate from priority for carbon funding. After the validation gate, propose a staged rollout. For each future nation or agreed area, first pin the official small-area index and area denominator, an evidenced estate inventory, applicable certificate and investment sources, data access terms and sample review design. Before any other area is selected or run, freeze the specification and Tower Hamlets baseline, review the tabletop outcomes and validate source coverage, identity and missingness on the existing pilot.
